## 2. Closest prior work: overlap vs difference

### 2.1 Hendrickson & Sela (2024), S1 (+ conference version S2) — **closest paper by a wide margin**

| | Overlap with IWS-RL | Difference from IWS-RL |
|---|---|---|
| Problem | Intermittent supply, equity among consumers, household storage tanks driving inequity | They model tanks inside the hydraulics with volume-driven demand (check valve + tank + pressure-driven discharge node per consumer). You describe "hidden" roof tanks; if yours sit outside the EPANET model, that is a simplification relative to theirs (I don't know your implementation — check this) |
| Control | Zone-level valves (one FCV per zone, 6 zones in their S-2 network) on a daily schedule | Their valves take **continuous** flow-limit settings; yours are **binary** open/closed per zone (16 actions) |
| Optimiser | An **optimised fixed open-loop schedule** (Bayesian optimisation, 100 iterations, scikit-optimize) — the same baseline class as your cross-entropy-tuned schedule | **No RL, no closed-loop control.** They explicitly assume no real-time tank-level data and re-solve the schedule per horizon from a forecasted supply schedule |
| Tooling | EPANET 2.2 via **WNTR**, pressure settings for partial/full demand | Their networks are a 6-consumer idealised network and a modified KY24 branched network (161 consumers); yours is EPANET Net3 with k-means zones |
| Scarcity | Their small network uses a source tank whose volume equals total daily demand — close in spirit to your daily water budget. Their medium network uses limited supply hours (pumps on for the first 8 h) | You use an explicit daily budget plus hourly control |
| Equity metric | Equity is the objective | Their supply equity is a uniformity-coefficient-style index (1 − mean absolute deviation of supply ratios, normalised); yours is the **minimum zone supply** (max-min) added to a −unmet-demand reward with weight λ |
| Robustness | They test the optimised schedule under ±50% randomised tank capacities and find it **still beats the uncontrolled baseline**; they also vary the supply schedule (4 scenarios) and find control efficacy falls as supply becomes more fragmented | They never compare the fixed schedule against an adaptive controller under shift. That gap is exactly where your findings 1–2 sit |
| Headline numbers | — | Medium network: supply ratio 0.79→0.87, supply equity 0.61→0.78 |

**Implication for you:** your Finding 1 (tuned fixed schedule beats plain PPO in-distribution) is consistent with, and strengthens, their framing. Your Finding 2 must be stated against their tank-size robustness result: they showed a fixed schedule degrades gracefully against *no control*; you show adaptive control beats the fixed schedule under *your* shifts. Both can be true.

### 2.2 Kurian et al. (2023), *Water Supply*, S5 — the "2023 MINLP paper"
- **Overlap:** intermittent rationed supply, valve schedule (constraints on supply hours and valve operation), objective is to **minimise inequity**; Indian rural network; also a data-driven variant using flow and level measurements; lab-scale experiments.
- **Difference:** mathematical programming (MINLP per the abstract), open-loop schedule, no RL. Whether household tanks are modelled is **UNVERIFIED** (full text not opened). Note: the search-result rendering of the DOI page listed "mixed integer linear programming" among keywords while the abstract says MINLP — check the full text before describing the formulation.

### 2.3 Kurian, Narasimhan & Narasimhan (2024), *Eng. Proc.* 69(1):184, S3 — "Kurian et al. (rural India)"
- **Overlap:** inequity under intermittent operation, pressure-driven analysis, head-end villages over-withdrawing.
- **Difference:** case analysis of one 107-village scheme in Central India; mitigation via bypass/shortened supply, booster pumps, isolating villages, air-lock analysis. No control optimisation, no RL.

### 2.4 Hu, Gao & Zhong (2023), *Water Supply*, S7
- **Overlap:** RL (MADDPG) for real-time **valve** (and pump) scheduling under demand uncertainty in an EPANET-type network; compared with GA/PSO/DE.
- **Difference:** continuous supply; objectives are energy and water-loss costs; no equity term; no household tanks; baselines are metaheuristics, not a tuned fixed schedule. (Snippet only.)

### 2.5 Siddique, Weng & Zimmer (2020), ICML, S6
- **Overlap:** RL that optimises a fairness welfare function across users (generalized Gini index); your λ·min term is an egalitarian scalarisation of the same family of concerns.
- **Difference:** no water domain. Their method optimises a fairness function of the vector of per-user returns; yours adds a min-zone term to a scalar reward once per day. You should **not** describe your method as "fair RL in the Siddique sense".

### 2.6 Hajgató et al. (2020), S8
- **Overlap:** DRL on EPANET; **demand randomisation at every episode reset** (random per-node multipliers plus a random total-demand scale) — a form of domain randomisation in water networks.
- **Difference:** instantaneous pump-speed set-point problem (no time dynamics), continuous supply, test scenarios drawn from the same randomisation distribution (no shift test), no equity, no tanks at households. Published journal version **UNVERIFIED** (I only read the arXiv version).

### 2.7 Gym4ReaL WDSEnv (2025), S9
- **Overlap:** EPANET RL environment with a binary on/off combinatorial action space (2^P pump combinations — structurally the same as your 2^4 valve combinations); training draws from a mixture of normal, stressful and extreme demand profiles; DQN compared against rule-based controllers.
- **Difference:** pumps not zone valves; continuous supply (Anytown); reward is demand satisfaction ratio plus overflow penalty, no equity; baselines are fixed/rule-based policies (including the `.inp` default tank-level rules), not an optimised open-loop schedule. Preprint.

### Q3. Is "an RL regime map showing when adaptive control beats a tuned fixed schedule, under shifts in demand and storage" new?

**Answer: Unclear. Confidence: low-medium.**

Evidence for "possibly new in this setting":
- The only IWS paper with an optimised fixed schedule plus storage-size perturbation (S1) compares that schedule against *no control*, not against an adaptive policy.
- Queries 2, 8, 14, 15 and 17 (§7) surfaced no IWS study comparing RL with a tuned fixed schedule across shift regimes.

Evidence against claiming novelty:
- The general idea — open-loop schedules suffice when conditions match the design, and feedback pays off under disturbance and model mismatch — is a long-standing control-theory point, not your discovery. Only the empirical map for this IWS setup could be new.
- **S18 (Wanjiru et al., 2016) already reports the qualitative version of your regime finding for household rooftop tanks under unreliable supply:** open-loop optimal control is adequate for steady demand; closed-loop MPC is more robust when the demand pattern changes. That is a single-household pumping-cost problem with MPC, not network equity with RL, but it means the *headline intuition* is not new. Cite it.
- S1 already shows (for fixed schedules) that control efficacy depends on supply-schedule regime and is somewhat robust to tank-size uncertainty, which is part of what a regime map would show.
- Database coverage limits (header) mean a relevant paper in ASCE/IWA/IEEE could have been missed.

## 5. Presentation wording

**Suggested novelty sentence:**
> "Building on the open-loop equity scheduling of Hendrickson & Sela (2024), we study closed-loop RL for zone valve control in intermittent supply with household storage. In our searches (listed in the report) we did not find prior work combining these elements. On EPANET Net3, a tuned fixed daily schedule was hard to beat in-distribution, while DQN and domain-randomised PPO outperformed it under the demand-surge, small-tank and scarce-supply shifts we tested — consistent with earlier open-loop vs closed-loop findings for household tanks (Wanjiru et al., 2016)."

Shorter slide version:
> "To our knowledge, based on a targeted search, this is an exploratory course study applying RL to equity-aware valve scheduling in intermittent supply; adaptive control helped under the shifts we tested, not in-distribution."

**Claims you must NOT make:**
1. "First" or "novel" RL for intermittent water supply, without the qualifier "in our search" and the query list.
2. "RL outperforms optimised schedules" without the qualifier "under the shifts we tested". In-distribution, your own result says the opposite.
3. "Robust" in general. You tested three shift types on one network.
4. That the method is "fair RL", "constrained RL" or "Lagrangian". Your λ·min term is a reward penalty, not a constraint, and not the welfare-function optimisation of Siddique et al. Also do not say "no one has used constrained RL for water equity" — S17 appears to have.
4b. That "adaptive beats fixed under shift, fixed wins in-distribution" is a new insight. The same qualitative pattern is reported for open-loop vs MPC on household tanks (S18) and is standard control intuition; your contribution is the RL/IWS-network evidence, not the idea.
5. "Near-optimal" based on the oracle. Your oracle shows little headroom on mean supply only; the gap on return and worst-zone supply means the policies are not near-optimal on those metrics.
6. That results transfer to real IWS networks. Net3 is not an intermittent network, and your zones are synthetic (k-means).
7. That Hendrickson & Sela's fixed schedule "fails" under uncertainty. They reported it still beat the uncontrolled baseline under ±50% tank-size perturbation.
8. "Statistically significant" unless you ran multiple seeds and a test. (Your 0.010 mean-supply drop and the −9.15 vs −9.67 return gap need seed variance to be interpreted.)
9. That your tank model matches the volume-driven demand literature, unless your tanks are actually inside the hydraulic simulation.
10. Any citation to "Cai et al. 2024" or an "npj Clean Water" paper until you locate and read it (see §6).
11. The 69/1/184 or 69/1/7 references for Hendrickson & Sela.
